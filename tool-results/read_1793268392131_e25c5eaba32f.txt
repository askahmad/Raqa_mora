   311→# 9. CRITICAL FINANCIAL CALCULATION RULES
   312→
   313→Treat these rules as business invariants and write unit tests for them.
   314→
   315→## Income
   316→
   317→Income increases the selected account balance and increases income totals.
   318→
   319→## Expense
   320→
   321→An expense decreases the selected account balance and increases expense totals.
   322→
   323→## Internal transfer
   324→
   325→A transfer decreases the source account and increases the destination account by the same amount.
   326→
   327→It is neither income nor expense. Never count an internal transfer twice in cash-flow reports.
   328→
   329→## Money lent
   330→
   331→When the user lends Rs. 10,000:
   332→
   333→- The selected cash/account balance decreases by Rs. 10,000.
   334→- The user's receivable increases by Rs. 10,000.
   335→- The amount is not recorded as an expense.
   336→- A corresponding person-wise obligation is created.
   337→
   338→## Partial repayment received
   339→
   340→If the borrower returns Rs. 4,000:
   341→
   342→- The selected account balance increases by Rs. 4,000.
   343→- The receivable decreases by Rs. 4,000.
   344→- The outstanding amount becomes Rs. 6,000.
   345→- The repayment is not recorded as new income.
   346→
   347→## Money borrowed
   348→
   349→When the user borrows Rs. 10,000:
   350→
   351→- The selected account balance increases by Rs. 10,000.
   352→- The user's payable liability increases by Rs. 10,000.
   353→- The amount is not recorded as income.
   354→
   355→## Repayment of borrowed money
   356→
   357→If the user repays Rs. 4,000:
   358→
   359→- The selected account balance decreases by Rs. 4,000.
   360→- The corresponding payable liability decreases by Rs. 4,000.
   361→- The principal repayment is not an expense.
   362→- Any interest or fee must be recorded separately as an expense.
   363→
   364→## Refunds and adjustments
   365→
   366→Refunds must correctly reverse or offset the original expense where appropriate.
   367→
   368→Balance adjustments require a reason, confirmation, and an auditable record.
   369→
   370→## Amount validation
   371→
   372→- Amounts must be positive and valid.
   373→- Repayments must not exceed the outstanding amount.
   374→- Transfers cannot have the same source and destination account.
   375→- A transaction must reference an account belonging to the authenticated user's financial space.
   376→- Concurrent repayment submissions must not create overpayments.
   377→- Duplicate submissions must be prevented where practical.
   378→
   379→## Financial precision
   380→
   381→Store monetary values in an integer-based smallest currency unit or another carefully chosen exact-decimal representation. For PKR, avoid binary floating-point arithmetic for authoritative financial totals.
   382→
   383→Build reusable utilities for:
   384→
   385→- Parsing user-entered amounts
   386→- Validating amounts
   387→- Formatting PKR
   388→- Adding and subtracting money
   389→- Computing account balances
   390→- Computing outstanding obligations
   391→- Computing period-based totals
   392→
   393→Never use formatted display strings as underlying monetary values.
   394→
   395→## Net financial position
   396→
   397→Use a clearly documented formula. At minimum:
   398→
   399→Net financial position = account balances + receivables − payables.
   400→
   401→Explain what is included or excluded. Do not describe an accounting estimate as the same thing as available cash.
   402→
   403→## Database integrity
   404→
   405→Use database transactions for financial operations that must succeed together. For example, creating a loan, creating its first money movement, and updating related records must not leave partially committed financial state.
   406→
   407→Enforce ownership and valid relationships in the database wherever practical. Do not rely exclusively on client-side checks.
