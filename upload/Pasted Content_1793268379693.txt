# 9. CRITICAL FINANCIAL CALCULATION RULES

Treat these rules as business invariants and write unit tests for them.

## Income

Income increases the selected account balance and increases income totals.

## Expense

An expense decreases the selected account balance and increases expense totals.

## Internal transfer

A transfer decreases the source account and increases the destination account by the same amount.

It is neither income nor expense. Never count an internal transfer twice in cash-flow reports.

## Money lent

When the user lends Rs. 10,000:

- The selected cash/account balance decreases by Rs. 10,000.
- The user's receivable increases by Rs. 10,000.
- The amount is not recorded as an expense.
- A corresponding person-wise obligation is created.

## Partial repayment received

If the borrower returns Rs. 4,000:

- The selected account balance increases by Rs. 4,000.
- The receivable decreases by Rs. 4,000.
- The outstanding amount becomes Rs. 6,000.
- The repayment is not recorded as new income.

## Money borrowed

When the user borrows Rs. 10,000:

- The selected account balance increases by Rs. 10,000.
- The user's payable liability increases by Rs. 10,000.
- The amount is not recorded as income.

## Repayment of borrowed money

If the user repays Rs. 4,000:

- The selected account balance decreases by Rs. 4,000.
- The corresponding payable liability decreases by Rs. 4,000.
- The principal repayment is not an expense.
- Any interest or fee must be recorded separately as an expense.

## Refunds and adjustments

Refunds must correctly reverse or offset the original expense where appropriate.

Balance adjustments require a reason, confirmation, and an auditable record.

## Amount validation

- Amounts must be positive and valid.
- Repayments must not exceed the outstanding amount.
- Transfers cannot have the same source and destination account.
- A transaction must reference an account belonging to the authenticated user's financial space.
- Concurrent repayment submissions must not create overpayments.
- Duplicate submissions must be prevented where practical.

## Financial precision

Store monetary values in an integer-based smallest currency unit or another carefully chosen exact-decimal representation. For PKR, avoid binary floating-point arithmetic for authoritative financial totals.

Build reusable utilities for:

- Parsing user-entered amounts
- Validating amounts
- Formatting PKR
- Adding and subtracting money
- Computing account balances
- Computing outstanding obligations
- Computing period-based totals

Never use formatted display strings as underlying monetary values.

## Net financial position

Use a clearly documented formula. At minimum:

Net financial position = account balances + receivables − payables.

Explain what is included or excluded. Do not describe an accounting estimate as the same thing as available cash.

## Database integrity

Use database transactions for financial operations that must succeed together. For example, creating a loan, creating its first money movement, and updating related records must not leave partially committed financial state.

Enforce ownership and valid relationships in the database wherever practical. Do not rely exclusively on client-side checks.
